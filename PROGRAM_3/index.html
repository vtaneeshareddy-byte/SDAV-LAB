🏭 Manufacturing Quality Control

Normal Distribution — Shaft Diameter

Industry problem: A factory produces shafts. The target diameter is 10 mm. Quality control requires the diameter to be between a lower specification limit (LSL) and an upper specification limit (USL). Shafts outside this range are defective. The diameter follows a normal distribution with mean μ and standard deviation σ.

🎛️ Adjust the parameters

f(x) = (1 / (σ√(2π))) · e−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)

☁️ Cloud Request Inter-arrival Time

Exponential Distribution

Industry problem: A cloud service receives requests according to a Poisson process with a constant average rate of λ = 12 requests per minute. The time between consecutive requests follows an exponential distribution. We want to model the probability of waiting less than a certain time for the next request.

🎛️ Adjust the parameters

📐 Formulas

f(t) = λ · e−λt   (t ≥ 0)
P(T ≤ t) = 1 − e−λt  |  P(T > t) = e−λt
P(T > t₂ | T > t₁) = e−λ(t₂−t₁)

Mean inter-arrival = 1/λ  ·  Variance = 1/λ²

📈 Exponential density & shaded probability

The shaded area represents P(T ≤ t). The right tail (unshaded) is P(T > t).

💡 Memoryless property: If you have already waited 2 seconds for a request, the probability of waiting an additional 3 seconds is the same as if you had just started waiting. The process "forgets" how long you have been waiting — because arrivals occur at a constant rate and are independent.

Note: This models request inter-arrival times, assuming a constant-rate Poisson arrival process. It does not describe server response time.
📦 Warehouse Processing Time

Central Limit Theorem

Industry problem: A warehouse processes orders one by one. Each order’s processing time has some distribution (not necessarily normal). The manager wants to know the distribution of the average processing time for a batch of n orders. The Central Limit Theorem tells us that as n grows, the sample average becomes approximately normal — regardless of the original distribution.

Input parameters

Interactive calculation will be enabled in the next step.

Mathematical statement

X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n)   as n → ∞
Standard error = σ / √n

Dynamic visualization

📈 Sampling distribution of the mean will appear here
Interpretation (preview): As you increase the sample size, the distribution of the sample mean becomes narrower and more bell-shaped. This is the magic of CLT — it lets us use normal-based methods even when raw data aren’t normal.
n: items per batch
